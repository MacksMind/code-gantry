## What the redraw taught

The previous draft of this stage was wrong about something, or it would have landed. Decide which of two things that is.

If it was **specific to this stage** — this scope was too narrow, this instruction was ambiguous — then it is spent by revising and there is nothing to record.

If it was **true of the plan, or of how a stage has to be drawn against this repository**, write it as a `plan_notes` entry, keyed to the plan item a later derivation will be reading. Nothing else carries it: the completed-stage history records what landed, not the drafts it took, and the cost table keeps the number of revisions without the reason for any of them. A lesson you leave in your reasoning is one the next derivation re-learns by spending another revision on it.
